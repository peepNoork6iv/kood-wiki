# Job interviews & how to ace them

Updated: October 22, 2025 4:41 PM

The process of finding a job may seem daunting. While it can take several months to complete all the rounds, it can also **be as short as a week** 💯

Generally, larger companies have more stages and a longer process, while smaller companies have a shorter and quicker process. The specific **process varies greatly from one company to another**. 

Think about the interviews as an opportunity to learn about the organisation and gain more experience and skills in navigating the hiring process 💪 Additionally, it's a positive sign when a company puts effort into choosing their employees - your new colleagues will be of high quality!

## Quick job interview basics

- To feel more relaxed before the interviews, try looking at the entire application process this way: this is also your opportunity to find out more about the workplace and a team, further, practice your interview skills 💪
- **Listen attentively**! Listening skill is a very important skill to demonstrate during the interview
- **Be positive**. For instance, don’t talk negatively about your previous employer or yourself.
- If the interview is online, **don’t do it from a car/public transportation 🚃** or anywhere with a distracting background.
- **Dress professionally**. No sweatpants, hoodies or shorts 🚫🩳🚫 smart casual is the way to go!
- **Arrive on time** 🕘 This immediately shows how (un)reliable you are and how important the interview is for you.
- Be friendly, polite and respectful to the interviewer, find a point of connection if possible.
- Do research about the interviewers - know who you’re dealing with!

Tip: if it’s an online interview put some post-its around your laptop to give you cues when answering the questions

# 🧿 HR/culture interview

This is usually the first interview after applying and if only an HR is present it will most likely not get technical. This type of job interview that focuses on assessing a candidate's fit with the company's culture and values, as well as their motivation, skills and experience.

Remember - every job interview is an experience that makes you more skilled in job interviews!

- **Prepare! Prepare! Prepare!** 📝 **Re-read the job description, research the company and its values**! Remember to relate what you talk about to what was describe in the job ad, but also make connections with the company and their mission. Cultural match is very important in modern companies, so it’s important to show that you have a similar worldview.
- Make sure you know **how to answer the most basic interview questions** (see the questions and possible answers in the next section). And don’t just think how you would answer, write down bullet points!
- Make sure to emphasise your motivation. As a junior you cannot rely on previous work experience, hence **attitude is everything**. Give examples like how many hours you’ve coded as a side hustle from your other responsibilities, tell them about the daily routine you set up for yourself and sticked to in order to finish the programme, etc. They want to see that your passionate and not just doing what you have to but also extra! Also just **be generally enthusiastic and positively energised**. Don’t be arrogant!
- Recruiters won’t like it if you give off the vibe that you are just **desperate for a job** and open to anything. Express that this exact job is what you want and give reasons for that.  **Show your interests for the position and the company, not finding a job!**
- **Don’t answer questions with yes/no**, talk rather more than less (by giving examples and evidence), but stay on the topic. Ideally a job interview is a dialogue.
- If you’re being honest about your shortcomings make sure to also explain how you are improving them - **what steps have you taken already.**
- Never talk negatively of your previous employer - they will think you’ll also talk negatively about your next company. If the relations didn’t end in good terms, don’t hide it, but **describe it as a lesson you learned from**.
- Use chatGPT for preparing, but don’t rely on it in terms of answers to give the interviewer (will be too generic and unoriginal). A good way to use chatGPT is to **insert the job ad and ask it to tell you typical questions a recruiter would ask you** and prepare the answers.
- Be **memorable**. If there’s an opportunity to connect with the recruiter on something (can be totally unrelated to the job), do it! They will remember it and like you more for it. Stay authentic, and be yourself. Show off your personality!
- If you feel that you’re not used speaking the language 🇬🇧 that the interview will be in practice beforehand to ensure you’re smooth even under pressure during the interview
- Make sure to **ask questions** during or in the end of the interview. Focus on the work itself, the team, the structure - things related to the work. Or for instance, growth opportunities within the company. Do not ask about remote work opportunities, benefits, or other organisational questions (unless you really need to). You will eventually know about these, but **don’t risk giving off the vibe that you only think about what the company can give to you, and not what you can give to the company**. Of course in reality it’s all about looking out for the number 1 which is you, but don’t let them know 😉
- **Salary** talks are likely to come up 💰 But the earliest to talk about this is the end of the first interview, not before, otherwise it’s bad taste. The recruiter probably wants you to say a number, but it’s better if they do. If they are reluctant to tell you, give them some ranges, usually this gives a hint. For instance, is the salary range between 1000-1500, 2000-2500 or 2500-3000 EUR? But just in case, have your number prepared as well. Always ask a bit more than you’d be OK with to leave room for negotiations. The current gross wage on the market for a starting Developer is currently between €2100 and €2700
- Even though the interview might not get technical, just in case freshen up your expertise and technical knowledge and skills
- Be ready to share your Github, LinkedIn, portfolio on the go during the interview
- Don’t be shy to show your personality and be authentic!

### **How to answer the most typical job interview questions**

- Tell us about yourself!

I assure you that this question will arise, and it can either be **the easiest or toughest question** depending on your level of preparation. I recommend preparing a 2-minute answer in which you describe your professional journey leading up to your current position. Be sincere, clear, and concise.

- Why do you want to work in this company?

Again, this question will almost definitely come up. Ensure that you research the company and **find compelling reasons why you want to work there**. Consider their values, mission, and any personal connections you may have. The goal is to demonstrate your motivation and genuine interest in working for this specific organisation.

- Why this role? What can you bring to this role?

Before the interview, review the job description and carefully consider why you are interested in the responsibilities of this role and why you want to join this team. Make sure to connect your answers to the specific requirements mentioned in the job description - recruiters appreciate that! For instance, if the job description emphasises the need for experience in JavaScript, discuss your experience and proficiency in JavaScript.

- Tell us about your achievements.

Think of **a few specific examples** when you felt proud of an achievement. It could be something like creating a homepage or solving a challenging programming problem. Make sure you can share concise and informative stories that portray you in a positive light. 

- Tell us about your projects.

Here as well, consider **a few particularly notable projects** you have completed and be prepared to discuss them. Provide concise and informative narratives about the projects, showcasing both your technical and soft skills.

- What are your expectations for this role?

Focus on what is **important to you and what you want for your future**. If you are considering a position at a large company, they may be able to assist you in selecting the appropriate team. For example, if you are applying for a junior developer role but there are openings in teams that specialise in fraud or finance, the recruiter can guide you in finding the right path.

- What are your weaknesses?

Please **avoid** providing answers such as "I work too hard" or "I'm a perfectionist" as they are not genuine and are not what the interviewer wants to hear. Instead, share a real weakness that is unrelated to the role. For example, if customer interaction is not required for the position you are applying for, you can mention that you are not particularly fond of customer-facing roles. Alternatively, you can mention a more general weakness such as feeling uncomfortable when giving a speech in front of a large audience.

- Where do you want to be in 2/5/10 years?

Many don’t know the answer to this one, and that is completely OK. However, I recommend making up one because **the interviewer probably wants to see that you are determined and systematically working towards a goal**. You don’t have to say you want to be in their company in 10 years. But try to think if you want a managerial position or become super advanced senior specialist. Or what do you want to specialise in - fintech, cybersecurity, video games etc?

**Other questions that may come up (gathered from technical recruiters and k/J students who have been to job interviews) and I recommend preparing for:**

- What are your expectations for your team/direct manager?
- Tell us about your experience studying in kood/Jõhvi
- How do you set and accomplish goals when under pressure?
- Have you ever faced conflict or disagreement? What did you do?
- Tell me about a time you failed. What happened and what did you learn?
- How do you handle unexpected challenges?
- Tell us about your experience with team work.
- How do you manage your time under pressure?
- Three words your friends would describe you as.

# 💻 Technical task & interview

Most technical positions will have some type of technical task or homework.

Depending on the company and position the tasks can vary to a large extent.

## **Bigger companies (such as Google, Meta, Bolt, Wise)**

- Are more likely to have **a clear path of interviews** and tasks that their recruitment process follows. Sometimes, there are multiple technical live tasks that you have to complete.
- **Platforms** such as [HackerRank](https://www.hackerrank.com/) are used to measure your code writing skills, problem-solving approach and motivation. Usually there is a developer from the company asking you to solve a problem, they are there talking to you and you can talk to them.
- The final technical interview is often about system design. **API design interview** usually aims to have a technical discussion with some of the Engineering Managers where you will cover the topics such as architecture and API design, as well as your experience and aspirations.
    
    Important to keep in mind for these types of live tests:
    
    - The engineer is there to see how well you can talk about your code
    - Ask questions - the engineer is there to help and even guide you. **The biggest mistake candidates do is to not gather enough information from the engineer**. For instance, if you don’t ask questions you cannot build a good system/API, so make sure to ask a lot of questions from the engineer!
    - For system design tasks focus on scaleability. Usually the companies that have that type of technical test are all about scaleability.
- These type of tasks usually take 1h-2h

### **How to prepare for such technical tasks?**

- To understand the interview process of the company you're interested in, you can visit their webpage. Big organisations often provide this information on their website, like [Meta](https://www.metacareers.com/swe-prep-onsite/) does. Alternatively, you can also find details about interview processes on other websites by doing a simple Google search. [Glassdoor](https://www.glassdoor.com/index.htm) is a useful resource that offers practice questions for some companies.
- Choose a coding language you are proficient in, unless the company has their requirement for the language to use.
- **Utilise platforms** like [Leetcode](https://leetcode.com/), [HackerRank](https://www.hackerrank.com/), and [HackerEarth](https://www.hackerearth.com/). **Practicing** on these platforms will enhance your chances of success in the actual test. Although you may code regularly, it is important to note that these questions **may be different** from your previous experience. By consistently engaging in these exercises, you can earn badges that can be showcased on LinkedIn, which some recruiters use for candidate searches.
- **Study** the topics that you know will be relevant
- Don’t jump into conclusions, the task might be harder than you think, **gather all the information from the engineer who is there**

**READ! 📚 The following books are highly recommended:**

- Cracking the Coding Interview by Gayle Laakmann McDowell
- System Design Interview by Alex Xu
- Designing Data-Intensive Applications by Martin Kleppmann

## Smaller companies

- Usually have **custom homework** created by someone from the team
- Often the homework is a task you do offline at home 🏡
- These are likely to be **about technologies mentioned in the job description** so you can already prepare by studying these before you receive the homework
- This type of homework **may take more time** than a few hours 🕤

# General must do’s for technical tasks/interviews

- Before answering a question, take a moment to think!
- Take some time to read programming theory — it will give you a solid vocabulary of commonly used terms and descriptions to get your point across and demonstrate your knowledge in the technical interviews.
- **The home task may not be as easy as it seems.** For instance, when a requirement is to use Angular, then also check what are the best practices and recent trends when using this certain technology - the aim of the home task is usually to see how you think, not just that you can create something that somehow works. Don’t just get it done, give it your best 💪 But also keep in mind that there is no one way to do these tasks correctly, usually they have several “right” ways.
- When it comes to the **live coding task**, the simple approach wins — just do what’s required.  It’s not necessary to show-off your knowledge and, as the end result, slow down the code, cause memory leaks, or edge cases.
- **Always ask questions** during or in the end of a technical interview! For example, what type of products are you building? How is the team structure?
- If someone asks you a question that you don't know the answer to, buy yourself some time by **stalling your response**. For example, you can say something like "good question" and then proceed to think out loud. **Instead of keeping your thoughts to yourself, make sure to share them. They are highly interested in your thought process, so it's important to demonstrate that even if you don't know the answer,** **you can still express and communicate your thoughts effectively 🗯️** This is an opportunity to showcase your ability to think creatively.
- You might be asked about the technical books you read, accounts you follow etc, prepare a few to demonstrate your interest in tech topics.
- Listen attentively!

# 🦹‍♀️ Final interview: Team Lead/Head of Department/CEO

This is typically just a “vibe check” and might be very similar to the HR/culture interview in the sense that the main goal is **getting to know you**. Hence, prepare similarly as you would for a HR/culture interview (see above).

Why are these interviews conducted? Especially in smaller companies, the executives want to make sure that they meet everyone who will become their employee. In bigger companies, it is often your future manager or your manager’s manager. 

**🧠 The way you introduced yourself shows managers how good of a summary you can give on something you know well, you.** 

Sometimes they haven’t looked at your CV and don’t know what you said in previous interviews, so **be prepared to repeat your answers 💬**

**Make sure to listen attentively and also ask questions** - it’s the final round ❕ If something is unclear, it's a good opportunity to get the answers you need. It's important to ask questions during every interview. If you can't think of anything, for example, you can ask about how the company dealt with the challenges of COVID-19 🦠 **Remember that this can be more like a discussion, as they are testing team fit.** 

Make sure to show your **motivation** and **enthusiasm** about the position and the company. Being passive or neutral at this stage might become a dealbreaker.

In this stage, the focus is typically on assessing culture and team fit. However, the interviewer **may still ask technical questions** if there were uncertainties regarding your technical skills during the process.

### Typical setup for this type of interview

1. Round of introductions on both sides
2. Quick overview on what the particular team or company is working on
3. Questions from the interviewer
4. Questions from you

## ❌ If you get rejected at any point

- It is completely normal, **everyone gets rejected**! The reasons can vary from a gut feeling of the Tech Lead to the company deciding not to hire anyone. Don’t beat yourself up!
- **Always remain on good terms - be polite and positive**. Even if there wasn’t a match this time, there might be an opportunity later on and you want to be on the recruiter’s shortlist.
- **Ask for feedback**! Recruiters often don’t provide any feedback but if asked directly what could be improved, they are mostly willing to help you with constructive feedback and tips.
