# Applying: GitHub, LinkedIn, CV, Cover Letter

Updated: October 22, 2025 4:42 PM

❗Don’t send your CV randomly to hundreds of companies! Instead apply less and customise your CV and cover letter. This will increase your chances!

❗Don’t get frustrated when you don’t get a job right away - it’s a matter of odds and luck as well. But this page helps you to increase these odds

❗Companies may not hire “junior” developers, just check the job ads and just “Software Developer/Engineer” positions. Moreover, if you are currently employed in a company that offers technical positions, securing your **first tech job becomes more attainable within the same organisation**. If possible you can also start by gradually incorporating technical tasks into your current role.

💪 Consider applying for a job even if you don't meet all the specified requirements in the job ad. This reminder is particularly crucial for women, as we often tend to undervalue our skills and capabilities.

❗Showing motivation and being pro-active is the most important thing when applying - if you do not hear back after weeks of sending your CV and cover letter, contact the company and let them know you’re interested!

# 🐱**GitHub**

Since you probably don’t have much work experience in IT, **recruiters will evaluate your technical skills based on your GitHub** - anyone can write in their resume that they have x, y, z technical skills, **the key is to demonstrate these skills**. 

### Profile README

- Create a **`README.md`** file for your GitHub profile by making a repository with your username (e.g., **`username/username`**). This serves as an introduction to visitors and can contain a brief about yourself, your skills and any projects you're proud of.
- Add relevant badges, gifs, or graphics to make it visually appealing.
- Also pin repositories that you want to showcase to your profile. This can be projects you've built or significant contributions you've made to other repositories.

### **Detailed Repository Documentation**

This is one of the more important indicators on your github. Other people should be able to understand what the repository is all about and how to go about it.

- Write a comprehensive **`README.md`**:
    - **Project Title and Description:** Briefly explain the project's purpose and functionality.
    - **Technologies Used:** List down languages, libraries, and tools used.
    - **Installation and Setup:** Provide a step-by-step guide on how to run the project locally.
    - **Screenshots/GIFs:** Visual representations can help others quickly understand your project.
    - **Contribution Guidelines:** If you're open to contributions, specify how others can contribute.
- Include a **`LICENSE`** (if applicable) and a **`.gitignore`** file.

### Other actions of note

- Organise with “Projects and Issues”
    - Use the "Projects" tab to manage and showcase your development process, especially for collaborative projects.
    - Use "Issues" to track bugs, enhancements, tasks, and more. Tagging issues and using milestones can demonstrate good project management skills.
- Star repositories that you find interesting or have learned from. This can give visitors an idea of your interests and the tech stack you're familiar with.
- Follow developers, organisations, or projects you admire. Engaging with their content or repositories can provide learning opportunities and increase visibility.
- Write clear commit messages. This showcases attention to detail and provides clarity for collaborators.

**Remember, your GitHub profile is a reflection of your technical journey. It's not just about showcasing your best work but also about demonstrating your growth, learning curve, and passion for technology. Keep it updated, and don't be afraid to showcase both your achievements and continuous learning ventures.**

Today LinkedIn is more important than CV, but works like a virtual CV. LinkedIn is where the large majority of IT positions are posted and filled. Further, recruiters source through LinkedIn. As a junior developer there is nothing more important when applying for a job than your LinkedIn. Hence, try to have **as much information on your LinkedIn profile as possible**.
❗**Don’t be afraid to talk to recruiters on LinkedIn!**

# LinkedIn essentials

**Everything in your profile should be in English** because recruiters almost always search using English keywords, plus tech world is in English, meaning recruiters speak English and may not speak your mother tongue!

- A profile photo - it doesn’t have to be super professional, more importantly it must be **up-to-date** and **show your face clearly.**
    - [How to create a profile photo in LinkedIn using AI](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/how-create-your-linkedin-profile-picture-ai-jan-tegze/)
    - Use frames (#opentowork) – or, you can apply that only visible for recruiters – then recruiters with LinkedIn Recruiter license will be able to see that you are open to work
- **Have a cover photo**, I recommend using [these](https://drive.google.com/drive/folders/10LukZvtVZ6JC-nZcP6ypUuKU4YlkMi0m) 🙂 If you want to make your own use [Canva.com](http://Canva.com) or [https://create.vista.com/](https://create.vista.com/)
- Add your **Headline**! This will be titled under your name at all times. Usually job title and employer (or student @ kood/Jõhvi for example) or you can write what you do or can do, for example, Full stack I CSS I JavaScript, for adding emojis use [emojicop](https://emojicopy.com/)
    - ‼ Recruiters use **keywords in their search for candidates (sourcing)**, so check the job ads, and **put the correct keywords**. No need to use words like junior or newbee, or unicorn, or ninja, or magician.
- **About section is mandatory**!
    - Try to be as **authentic** as possible - **don’t use many buzzwords or let chatGPT write it** - you’ll end up with something generic and boring that recruiters won’t read
    - Be honest about your experiences
    - Include your **interests**, mainly professional interests/goals/achievements but definitely share some personal passions as well, the more unique the better because this is part of your brand and makes you special. However, avoid getting too intimate and personal
    - In addition, it is an opportunity to show your **technical knowledge** from the angle of your experiences/projects/interests
    - Put in keywords and phrases that apply to the role you want to have
    - Use Grammarly extension for proof reading
    - You can find some good examples [here](https://www.linkedin.com/business/talent/blog/product-tips/linkedin-profile-summaries-that-we-love-and-how-to-boost-your-own) or just check other profiles, industry experts for example.
- **Verify your email** - this shows recruiter that you’re a real person
- Make sure you have your **location and contact info** added. This makes it easier for recruiters to find you and contact you.
- **Work experience 🏢** Make sure to include in the description 1-2 sentences about what the company does. Also include your accomplishments when you were working there. If accomplishments are hard to describe, list your responsibilities as bullet points so they’re easy to read. Recruiters look not just on your technical experiences, but **also your soft skills**. So don’t be shy to put your customer service roles or any other irrelevant to IT industry roles. ❗️**Remember, every experience counts!**
- **Education**. Mention kood/Jõhvi but also your previous education 🎒 For kood/Jõhvi keep in mind that most people don’t know what makes the programme special, so describe kood/Jõhvi and your experience in a few sentences and also your technical skills + soft skills that have you gained, for instance, **one of the biggest strengths of k/J students is that you have elaborate team work experience - mention that**!
- In other education, if you finished with cum laude or did something special during your time at school/university, mention this as well - the more the merrier! Here as well use keywords and skills, and can even involve subjects you have studied.
- **Volunteer** experience. Volunteering shows that you also care about a specific cause, not just money, and it shows you’re an active motivated individual 🏋️ Plus it gives more topics to talk about (and relate) during the interviews, and **shows you don’t only do what you have to, but do things out of interest and passion**.
- **Skills 🛠️** Add here skills that are relevant for the type of positions you’re applying for. It’s good to have your technical skills here (e.g. Figma, Adobe Suite, Tableau, Python, Jira, etc.) as well as other skills (e.g. data analytics, user research, project management, customer service, leadership, team work). This is like social proof. Ask the people you’ve worked with to endorse these skills. For example, fellow kood/Jõhvi students.
- **Interests**. Make sure to include here people and companies you’re interested in, especially **the company you’re applying for** (and not their competitors unless you’re applying for both hehe)
- If you would like to be contacted by recruiters then make sure you do and **share the certificates you complete on LinkedIn** - some of our students have been contacted once they have started doing this!
- **Languages**. Make sure to add, these are important to know for any position.

# 🧑 **CV**

### What to include in your CV?

- Ideally, your CV should be **tailored to each job you apply for**, and be as relevant for the position and specifically for the job description as possible. Include the **same key words as were used in the job ad** - the company’s first selection filter might be automatic (AI)
- Add a small paragraph in the beginning of your CV describing very shortly who you are, your journey to this point and future interests - already here there should be a match with the job ad
- As you probably do not have much previous experience working in tech, create a **skills based resume not work experience based resume**, meaning focus on your technical skills and demonstrate you have these skills by giving evidence - for example explain a project you have done in kood/Jõhvi or independently. You may also link it (github/portfolio) to your CV to save space.
- **Any higher education** regardless of the field, because you never know why it may be a plus for the employer, also the skills you gained during acquiring the education are transferable
- Work experience - **include it even if it is not directly connected to the position you are applying for**, for example if it is a customer facing role, such as waiter, can be a great plus because it shows you have you can deal with people which is a benefit in any job position. However, do not use up too much space if it is a non-tech position. **Try to avoid having long caps in your CV where you have neither studied nor worked**
- Contact info: email, **LinkedIn**, phone number
- Location: town/region where you live and if you are willing to relocate
- Languages you speak and on which level
- Any certifications
- Include things you have done that were **extra** - **shows you don’t only do what you have to, but do things out of interest and passion**. Technical experiences are the best, but also include other such as being part of the student council at school or working at soup kitchens during weekends. It also gives some interesting content for the recruiter to ask you about.

### Tips for CV

- Use **a template** to create your CV, for example, [Canva.com](http://Canva.com) - they have many well designed CV templates to use for free
- Think about your CV from the perspective of the recruiter, meaning, include the most relevant things (relevant job experience, projects you’ve completed) in the beginning, and less relevant things in the end/smaller font
- Use clear, concise language and make sure your formatting is consistent and **easy to read**
- Your CV should ideally be 1-2 pages.
- Send your CV as PDF - some companies have automatic systems looking through the CVs, and this is the most compatible file format.
- Make sure your CV and cover letter match in terms of the information they provide

# 📹 Video CV

Some positions get hundreds of applicants and one way for a recruiter to manage the workload is to let the candidates make a video recording of themselves answering to a few questions. **Meaning, the main idea is to filter out the ones who wouldn’t make the effort. And in reality, the drop-off here is always significant, so if you already make the video, you have a good chance!** 

I know it can be daunting to send a video of yourself, so here are few tips:

- Prepare what you will say before you start recording, **but don't read off your notes**, rather have some cues for yourself, like keywords to have a glance at while recording. This will make the video more natural and generally better 🍃
- Take your time. **Record as many times as it takes**. Make most of the pre-recorded video format.
- Show your ✨ enthusiasm and motivation ✨ I know it can be unnatural if you’re an Estonian, but **if you're not excited about the role, they won't be excited about you**
- Remember that everyone is in the same boat, **others who apply won't be professional YouTubers 🤳** they'll be just like you, so don't be too hard on yourself!
- Make sure **you don't have any distractions** for the people who watch the video, for example, try to have a rather neutral background, make sure they can see and hear you clearly etc.

# 📜 **Cover Letter**

- **❗PLEASE don’t let ChatGPT write your cover letter!** 🙅🙅🙅 The recruiter can tell right away and it’s a huge minus. Instead, write your own thoughts and ideas, and keep it simple. Also **don’t use templates of generic cover letters you find online**. Be authentic, that’s when you’re the most impressive!
- Even if the job ad is only asking for a CV, include a customised cover letter because writing a cover letter even when you don’t have to is the **first demonstration of motivation,** and motivation is the most important thing to demonstrate!
- Focus on what **you can give** to the company, not on what the company can give you! And **why** you want to work in this company and in this position specifically
- Use the job description to **identify** **the key skills the employer is looking for ⚒️** and explain how your experience and skills match those requirements
- Keep your cover letter **concise** and to the point, and make sure to **proofread** it carefully before submitting your application to avoid grammar and other mistakes
- If you don’t have some of the skills or experience described in the job ad, a cover letter is a good place to explain how you are not there yet but how you are planning to get there or how you are on your way to get there
- Why not attach a recommendation with your application as well? Let someone who has worked with you to write a banging **recommendation letter** and if possible add this as well
- **Add a customised portfolio of your projects!**
- Make sure your CV and cover letter match in terms of the information provided
