### ⁉️**Why Reviews Matter**

### Essential for Peer-to-Peer Learning

Reviews in our system are not just assessments; they are opportunities for development and growth for both reviewees and reviewers. This process involves exchanging ideas, understanding differing perspectives, and nurturing an environment of mutual growth.

### Feedback for Progress

Our curriculum aims to develop problem-solving skills and attitudes conducive to effective teamwork in intelligent work collectives. Reviews offer a chance to learn from each other's approaches and mistakes, fostering collective intelligence.

### Consequences of non-participation in reviews

If you fail to take part of review or your participation in review process has been weaker than anticipated, you can receive a behavior flag. For behavior flag you can receive a strike or a written warning. 3 strikes will give you written warning and 3 written warnings will terminate you from study program. More information about behavior flags can be read [here](Peer%20Evaluations,%20Inactivity%20Days,%20Behaviour%20Flags%20278440b4458b80de9d5be9081a2267c7.md). To avoid getting behavior flags use inactivity days when you know that you can’t participate in reviews for more than 3 days.

### **Roles & Responsibilities in Peer-to-Peer Evaluations**

**Reviewer Responsibilities**

- Conduct fair and thorough reviews. There is as much for you to learn as there is for the reviewees.
- Test the team's work against the review criteria, giving clear and constructive feedback on each aspect.
- Engage in meaningful discussions, exploring different methodologies and approaches.

**Reviewee Responsibilities**

- Be present with your team and actively participate in the review process.
- During the review session, clearly explain the project's objectives, methodologies, and individual contributions.
- Approach feedback as a learning opportunity. Consider critiques thoughtfully and constructively, using them to refine your approach and enhance your skills.

### **Conducting a Productive Review**

1. **Preparation and Team Assembly**:
    - Once the project team submits their work, a review team is automatically created. The size of the review team is proportional to the project team to ensure comprehensive coverage.
    - A total of one week is given to complete the review process.
2. **Understanding the Review Components**:
    - The review process is divided into functional and qualitative questions, accessible on the platform.
    - **Functional Questions**:
        - Aim to assess whether the project meets the necessary functional criteria.
        - All functional questions need to be passed for the project to be considered successful. If any are not passed, the project team will have to revise and fix their code.
        - Successfully answered functional questions contribute to 60% of the final project score.
    - **Qualitative Questions**:
        - Evaluate the quality of the project, including adherence to good coding principles.
        - The answers to these questions determine 40% of the final score.
3. **Conducting the Review Session**:
    - A review session between the project and review teams is mandatory.
    - All members should be present, with the session lasting between 15 to 60 minutes.
    - The session's purpose is for the review team to gain a deeper understanding of each team member's contribution and to provide immediate, live feedback on the project.
    - The discussion should focus on the project's objectives, methodologies, and implementation, allowing for a comprehensive evaluation and exchange of ideas.
4. **Review Session Guidelines**:
    - Schedule a dedicated time for the session, ensuring all team members can attend.
    - Engage in a detailed discussion about the project’s objectives, methodologies, and code quality.
    - Reviewers should ask questions to clarify any uncertainties and provide explanations for any changes they suggest.
    - Check for code quality, understandability, modularity, and efficiency, adhering to the project's coding standards and best practices.
5. **Feedback and Reflection**:
    - Conclude the review process with a 360 feedback session, allowing both teams to reflect on their work and provide feedback to each other.
    - Team captains have the opportunity to give additional feedback to members of the other team, fostering an environment of continuous learning and improvement.

### **Process and Expectations**

- Evaluations are conducted in groups, with a specific size range.
- A timeframe of 7 days is allocated for each review cycle.
- If you know you won’t be able to conduct reviews for some time then inactivity must be reported, with a minimum permissible period being 4 days.

### **Consequences of Non-Participation**

- Non-participation without prior notification leads to the reviewer receiving a grade of ‘X’.
- 3 repeated non-participations for the reviewer will result in termination from the program.

## **✅ Steps for a Constructive Peer-to-Peer Evaluation**

1. **Preparation:** Schedule and plan the review session, ensuring all participants are informed.
2. **Participation:** All team members must be present and engaged in the process - both the reviewing team and the project team.
3. **Discussion:** Start with understanding the project’s objectives and contributions from each team member.
4. **Review:** Go through the project, matching the outcomes with the set criteria.
5. **Feedback:** Provide constructive feedback, focusing on learning and improvement.

## **❌ When a Review is Considered Unsuccessful**

- Project team is not able to explain the objective of the project and the way it was globally implemented
- Project team is not able to explain the choices that they made with algorithms, technologies, tools
- A member in the project team is not able to explain their contribution to the project and how their code was built
- The project does not meet the expected outcomes as per the review criteria.
