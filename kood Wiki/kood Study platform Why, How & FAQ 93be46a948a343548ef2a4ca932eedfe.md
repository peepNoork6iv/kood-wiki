## Study

This is the place for all the active and finished modules, with all their content. 

- All the opened and finished modules will appear on the left, with the deadlines of the modules visible for each
- If the module is already finished, you can still access all the task descriptions and team information, but you won’t be able to submit new tasks
- Each topic is divided into topics, and each topic into tasks. Think of topics as projects, and tasks as parts of the project
- Topics can have deadlines, **make sure to check what the deadline for the specific topic is**. For the Go module of Batch 4, all the topics have the same deadline (22 July 2024), which means that all the mandatory tasks need to be submitted by that date.
- You can access all the available task descriptions, but you have to do the topics one by one, starting from the top-most. Once the mandatory tasks from the first topic are finished, the second topic will open up, and so on.

## **Workflow and Communication**

### **Workflow**

While we recommend adopting a workflow that best suits your project's needs, here are two methodologies you might consider. These approaches are very popular at the market as well so it is best to familiarise yourself with these ideas

- **Agile Methodology:** Agile, particularly Scrum or Kanban, is recommended for its flexibility and efficiency in managing tasks. This involves regular stand-ups, sprint planning, and retrospectives to adapt to changing project needs quickly
- **Waterfall Methodology (Alternative for Long-Term Projects):** For projects with a longer timeline and well-defined stages, the Waterfall methodology can be effective. This approach involves completing each phase of the project (such as conception, analysis, testing, implementation) in sequence. It's beneficial for projects with clear objectives and stable requirements.

## **Project Management**

- **Tools:** Choose from tools like Trello, Jira, or Asana for tracking tasks and milestones. The selection should depend on the team's preference and project complexity.
1. **Project Planning:** Starting with a detailed planning session is crucial to define the project's scope, objectives, and timelines. This foundation will guide the team's efforts and help manage expectations.
2. **Documentation:** Keep comprehensive documentation for your project, making it accessible and understandable for others and for your future reference.

## Passing or Failing a Task

- There are two possible outcomes for a task: you either pass or fail.
- If you PASS, you’ll get your points and can move on to the next mandatory task
- If you FAIL:
    - The task needs to be resubmitted after fixing the mistakes
    - The admin can take note of the failed task status and inquire about the reasons behind it

## Project Deadlines

- Tasks inherit the topic’s deadline, which means that in order to gain points for the tasks, they need to be submitted and approved before the topic’s deadline.

# Peer Reviews

## Why Peer Reviews?

- **They power peer learning:**
In a teacherless environment, peer reviews are an effective way of ensuring that projects have been correctly implemented.
- **Preparation for the workplace:**
Code-review is a crucial step towards ensuring that submitted code does what it is supposed to do. Peer-review is great preparation towards being ready to perform this function in the workplace.
- **Share best practice:**
It’s an opportunity to collaborate with people that you may not overlap with. The reviewers and students-under-test can share best practices, and both learn from the experience.

For more information on why reviews matter, please visit [](https://app.notion.com/p/b7e4ba697b06495eadacf988628cb14c?pvs=21) 

## Giving a Review

### How to Give a Good Review?

- **Provide honest quality feedback:**
Nobody likes to give bad news, but providing honest and quality feedback is a helpful step in the development of the students-under-test. The goal of the reviewer is to ensure that the students-under-test have implemented a project which satisfies the objectives of the task description. It’s an important step to ensure that students only move on, once they have developed the necessary skills for that section of the curriculum. Ultimately, ensuring that students graduate with the correct graduate profile.
- **Read the task:**
To ensure that the review runs smoothly, read the task to ensure that you have your best understanding of it.
- **Check knowledge:**
Ensure that all members of the student group understand the task.
- **Inspect the code:**
Ensure that the student group has fully implemented the project using only the allowed packages and libraries, and that all code is their own.
Goal of review: **ROLES: REVIEWER + STUDENT**
- **Build and run the code:**
To ensure that it does what it is supposed to do. Ensure that the project properly solves the problems described in the task description.
- **Answer all of the review questions:**
They’re important, don’t skip any. Follow the review question’s steps if there are any.
- **Retest after code changes:**
It’s easy to introduce new bugs while changing code. Make sure the project still works after any changes have been made.
- Once you’re assigned a review, you can access the review under the ‘Reviews’ tab in Nav bar
    
    ![Visible from the ‘Reviews’ tab on the left navigation bar](kood%20Study%20platform%20Why,%20How%20&%20FAQ/Untitled.png)
    
    Visible from the ‘Reviews’ tab on the left navigation bar
    
    ![Review team view of the review card before Check-in](kood%20Study%20platform%20Why,%20How%20&%20FAQ/Screenshot_2024-03-20_at_10.55.33.png)
    
    Review team view of the review card before Check-in
    
- The review card will show the names of the submitting team, as well as the names of the review team
- The project can be accessed through the Gitea project repository link, provided on the card.
- Each review team member has 72 hours to check-in to the review. This means going to the review card and clicking on the ‘Check-in’ button. Fail to check in? You'll be removed from the review, ensuring that submitters will not get stuck behind unfinished reviews for weeks.
- The review team has a total of 7 days to review the task, starting from the submission time.
- The reviews are to be conducted in groups, meaning that the review team has to **communicate with each other** about the division of review stories or discussion about assessing different stories. We recommend using Discord for that.

![All reviewers need to ‘Approve’ the current review state. You can check the status of fellow reviewers next to their name for easier communication.](kood%20Study%20platform%20Why,%20How%20&%20FAQ/Screenshot_2024-03-20_at_11.00.54.png)

All reviewers need to ‘Approve’ the current review state. You can check the status of fellow reviewers next to their name for easier communication.

- Please make sure you **don’t finish the review solo** without discussing the work with your peer reviewer. Establish communication with each other within 24h from the start of the review process, and make a plan on how to collectively give the review.
- Each task is to be run and thoroughly tested by the review team, making sure that the code does what it’s supposed to do and what the stories ask it to do.
- If any bugs or shortcomings are observed, the review team should let the project team know so they could address the issue. During the 7 day review period, the project team can fix the bugs and improve on the code (if necessary), in order to pass the task. This back and forth requires good communication between the project and the review team. We recommend using Discord for that.
- If you’ve gone over the review stories, hit ‘Approve’ button in the bottom to let the submitter as well as the fellow reviewers know that you approve of the current review state. All reviewers need to approve of the review state before the submitter can accept the review.
- If other reviewers modify the review stories, it will revert the approve statuses for all. This ensures that reviewers cooperate and ensure a fair review process with the most optimal and fair final score.

![Describe the issues inside the comment box, and hit ‘Approve’ when you think you’re done](kood%20Study%20platform%20Why,%20How%20&%20FAQ/Screenshot_2024-03-20_at_11.09.47.png)

Describe the issues inside the comment box, and hit ‘Approve’ when you think you’re done

- If you decline a user story or give sub-maximum scores, make sure to explain the issue at hand inside the comment box.
- We encourage the review team to be thoughtful, thorough and polite when doing the review. Good communication is key here!
- Within the 7 days, you can always come back to the unfinished review as the last state will be saved.
- As part of the review process, you must conduct a 15-45 minute review session between all the members of project and review teams in order to:
    - give the project team the opportunity to introduce their solution and present their work
    - understand better what the code does
    - design decisions behind the code
    - understanding and comprehension of the code between all the project team members (e.g. “What does this block of code do?”)
- Reviews are key, no matter your familiarity with the project. Here’s how you can make a meaningful contribution as a reviewer:
    - **If you've already completed the task:**
        - Confirm the team has met all requirements by referring to review questions.
        - Provide constructive feedback and share your knowledge.
        - Organize a call (15-45 min) to discuss the work, ensuring the team understands their solutions and coding decisions.
    - **If the task is new to you:**
        - Don’t worry if the project is unfamiliar! Use the review questions to guide you.
        - Set up a call (15-45 min) for the team to explain their work and clarify any doubts. Your questions will also prepare you for when you tackle this task yourself.

## Review Deadlines

- Each review team member has **72 hours to check-in** to the review. This means going to the review card and clicking on the ‘Check-in’ button.
    - Fail to check in? You'll be removed from the review, ensuring that submitters will not get stuck behind unfinished reviews for weeks.
- The review team has **7 days** from the start of task submission to finish the review. This includes:
    - Evaluating the review stories
    - Setting up and conducting a 15-45 min review session (see [Giving a Review](kood%20Study%20platform%20Why,%20How%20&%20FAQ%2093be46a948a343548ef2a4ca932eedfe.md))
- We **strongly** encourage the review team to get their team together **no later than 24h** after the start of the review process, in order to give ample time for possible code improvements and communication with the project team.
- Both the project and the review team have **7 days** from the review acceptance to finish the feedback round

## Not Participating in a Review

- Not participating in the review process will result in a warning. This includes:
    - not checking in for the review
    - not participating in the review process even though the review has been assigned to you
    - not participating in the feedback round
- Three warnings will give the school the right to terminate your studies in the program.

# FAQ

## Group Projects and Teamwork

1. **Finding Group Members**:
    - "How does the platform address the challenge of matching students at different progress levels for group tasks?"
        
        A student will be able to select the group from the people that have completed all the mandatory tasks up to the task in hand, and that haven’t yet started on this task. Self-paced study means that faster and slower students might not get the chance to work together, unless the faster student spends some time on bonus tasks in order to wait for the other to catch up. 
        
    - "Can two group leaders collaborate or invite each other to form a group?"
        
        Currently, the only way to form a group is via the leader’s invitations. Requesting to join someone else’s group is currently under development.
        
2. **Group Dynamics**:
    - "What steps can be taken if there's a dispute within a group or disagreement over a project's direction?"
        
        When encountering disputes within a group or disagreements over the direction of a project, it's essential to address these challenges constructively. Here are a few ways to consider:
        
        - **Democratic Decision-Making**: Adopt a structured approach that involves:
            - **Voting**: Every team member gets a vote after a structured discussion on the disputed topics.
            - **Timed Discussions**: Set a specific time to discuss each topic, allowing everyone to voice their opinions.
            - **Resolution by Majority**: Once discussions conclude, a vote determines the course of action, ensuring decisions are made democratically.
        - **Mediation and Facilitation**: In cases where voting may not be suitable, seek the help of a neutral third party or facilitator to mediate discussions. This can help clarify misunderstandings and guide the group towards a consensus.
        - **Regular Check-ins**: Schedule regular meetings to discuss project progress and address any emerging concerns before they escalate into larger disputes.
        
        It’s important to maintain open communication, mutual respect, and fairness, aiming to resolve disagreements in a way that strengthens the team's cohesion and fosters a productive working environment.
        
    - "Are there any guidelines or support for managing group dynamics and ensuring effective collaboration?"
        
        First of all we’ll direct you to some recommendations on how to work in groups: [Teamwork](kood%20Study%20platform%20Why,%20How%20&%20FAQ%2093be46a948a343548ef2a4ca932eedfe.md).
        
        To conclude some information then each group/project has a captain who should be responsible for managing their group and making sure the project is coming along. If there problems within your group which you can’t solve on your own, you can always contact kood/Support on discord or by email (support@kood.tech)
        
3. **Project Submission and Management**:
    - "What happens if a project is submitted accidentally? Is there a way to revert the submission?"
        
        It will be difficult to submit a project accidentally since you will have to confirm the submission. Once the project is submitted, you will have 7 days to fix any errors that might occur.
        
    - "Are there any penalties for failing a project or not meeting project deadlines?"
        
        The admin can take note of the failed task status and inquire about the reasons behind it. If the failures are repetitive and caused from lack of effort, the school has a right to give out a warning. Three warnings will give the school the right to terminate the student’s studies.
        
    - “If a new module has opened up for me, can I still submit tasks for points from the previous module?”
        
        If the module end time has not passed, one can still submit tasks and receive points for it, even if a new module has opened up. 
        

## Peer Reviews

1. **Review Process**:
    - "What should I do if I'm assigned to review a project that is more advanced than my current level?"
        
        Don’t panic. Read the task to try to get the best understanding of it before the review begins. Try to follow the normal flow of a review. If something is unclear, the students-under-test should be able to explain it to you. Finally, trust yourself and don’t be intimidated. Most of the review questions are to use the project from the perspective of a user of the system. They’re designed to be achievable for anyone.
        
    - "How are review assignments determined? Will I review peers from my study module or from other batches?"
        
        Currently, reviews are assigned within the same batch. These can include different modules.
        
2. **Review Challenges and Disputes**:
    - "What can be done if a review is perceived as incorrect or unfair?"
        
        If the project team and the review team have not been able to agree on the review result, the project team should contact the school administrator.
        
    - "Is there a process to dispute or discuss a review if personal biases are suspected?"
        
        If personal biases are suspected, please contact the school administrator for support. 
        
3. **Post-Review Actions**:
    - "Do reviewers get notified when a project they reviewed is updated with bug fixes or changes?"
        
        There is currently no automatic notification set up. This means the project team should notify the review team of the updates and bug fixes to the project.
        
    - "How should a reviewer proceed if the project they're reviewing has significant updates or fixes?"
        
        It is okay for the project team to update the code and fix bugs based on the review team feedback. However, if the review team has communicated the issues in advance and the fixes come in with too little time left to conduct a review session and assess the changes, the review team has the right to leave the assessment as is.
        
4. **Review Scheduling and Deadlines**:
    - "Can a review be assigned if I am approaching an inactivity period?"
        
        Review can only be assigned if you have more than 7 days until the start of your inactivity period.
        
    - "How are review deadlines set, and what happens if they're not met?"
        
        Not participating in the review process will result in a warning. This includes:
        
        - not participating in the review process even though the review has been assigned to you
        - not participating in the feedback round
        
        Three warnings will give the school the right to terminate your studies in the program.
